Preparation Tips

    FOOD & PANTRY PREPAREDNESS:

    Build a practical pantry using food your household already eats and regularly rotates. Useful options may include:

  • Ready-to-eat canned food
  • Crackers and other shelf-stable snacks
  • Food requiring little water or cooking


    • BASIC HEAT PRECAUTIONS

  • Drink water regularly instead of waiting until you feel very thirsty.
  • Stay in the shade when possible.
  • Reduce strenuous outdoor activity during the hottest parts of the day.
  • Wear lightweight and loose-fitting clothing.
  • Check on children, seniors, people with chronic medical conditions, people who live alone, and pets.
  • Use fans, but only if indoor temperatures are less than 32°C. In temperatures above 32°C, a fan can increase body temperature.

    • HEAT EXHAUSTION SIGNS:

  • High internal body temperature (above 40°C)
  • Dizziness or fainting
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Reduced urination
  • Seizures

    • HEAT STROKE FIRST-AID:

  • Move the person out of the sun or heat to a cooler spot.
  • Pour water over their skin and use anything available to fan them.
  • Soak a towel in ice water and apply it to their skin. Cover as much of their skin’s surface area as possible. Replace with newly soaked towels every minute or so.
  • DO NOT give the person fever-reducing medication, like acetaminophen. These medications don’t help heat stroke, which happens for different reasons than a fever. They may also worsen complications like organ damage.

    • CURTAINS & WINDOW HEAT CONTROL

  • Close curtains or blinds before strong direct sunlight reaches the window.
  • Use properly sized room-darkening or blackout curtains on windows that receive strong sun.
  • Shade windows from the outside when this can be installed safely.
  • Open windows when outdoor air is cooler and security and outdoor air quality permit.
  • Close windows and other openings when outdoor air is hotter than the room.

    • FOOD SAFETY DURING A BROWNOUT: FRIDGE, FREEZER & COOLER GUIDE

    When the power goes out:

  • Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed.
  • If the doors stay closed, food will stay safe up to: 4 hours in a refrigerator, 48 hours in a full freezer, 24 hours in a half-full freezer.
  • If the power has been out for 4 hours, and a cooler and ice are available, put refrigerated perishable foods in the cooler. To keep them at 40°F or below, add ice or a cold source like frozen gel packs.

    • Community Care

    • The Project LAWA (Local Adaptation to Water Access)
      • at BINHI (Breaking Insufficiency through Nutritious Harvest for the Impoverished) focuses on addressing water scarcity and food insecurity in impoverished communities. The project promotes local adaptation strategies to improve access to water and increase food production through sustainable farming practices.
    • Pilot Heat Action Plan Interventions Activity
      • Quezon City Government is an activity brought together workers, health and school staff, barangay representatives, and local officials to discuss extreme-heat risks and safety measures. Their experiences will help the city develop responses that meet residents’ needs, especially those of outdoor workers.
